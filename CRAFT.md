# CRAFT -- the finish rules for everything we build (Colin, Nov 2 2026)

> *"Things geometrically that just don't look designed very well, and my attention to detail says that needs to be corrected... When water comes out
> of stuff it's always a back-and-forth getting it not to look cheesy; railings attached to posts or stairs are floating; things attached to trees or
> plants float. The biggest thing: when things are supposed to be ORGANIC, like plants, having a 100% straight edge just looks insane."*

Every builder (every worker, every zone) checks its work against these BEFORE shipping, on a close render, not only a wide one.

## 1. Organic things are never straight
Vines, ivy, roots, branches, hanging moss, rope, grass, leaves, streams, waterfalls, cloth: no perfectly straight edge or line, ever.
- Build them as CURVES (a spline with a sag or droop, gravity-shaped), with a TAPER (thick at the root, thin at the tip) and a little noise.
- Hanging things (ivy, vines, lantern cords) droop in a catenary and sway; they never stick out stiffly.
- Several strands vary in length, thickness and phase, never copies side by side.

## 2. Water comes FROM somewhere
A stream or fall never begins as a line out of a hill.
- It starts at a SOURCE: a spring pool, a crack in the rock with wet dark stain round it, a spout or pipe, a lip.
- It leaves the lip in an ARC (a ballistic curve, not a vertical sheet) and widens as it falls; it lands in something (a pool, spray, mist, foam).
- Edges are broken (torn, clumped, streaky), the top is the brightest, there is spray and a little mist at the bottom.

## 3. Everything that touches, TOUCHES
No floating attachments, no gaps, no interpenetration you can see.
- Railings sit ON their posts; posts sit ON the deck or the ground (a base, a footing).
- Stairs rest on something: stringers, a solid mass, the ground; each tread meets the next.
- Things on trees (ivy, lanterns, a sword peg, a hammock rope) are attached at a real point on the bark: wrapped, tied, pegged.
- Props meet the ground with contact: a slight sink, a base, a shadow; nothing hovers by a few centimetres.

## 4. Materials never go black
- A fully metallic material shows only its reflections: give every metal (the bell, gold trim, bronze) an environment that is ALWAYS present, or
  a lower metalness with a warm floor. A black bell is a bug.
- Undersides and back faces read as rock with light on them (ambient/bounce, a rim, vertex AO), not as black holes.
- Nothing is shown before its textures have loaded (warm before reveal; cut-scenes and renders included).

## 5. Show it so it can be read
- A shot or a view shows its subject whole, front-on or 3/4, unoccluded, at a distance where you can tell what it is (CINEMA.md house style).
- If a close-up does not read in one glance as WHAT it is, it is the wrong shot.

## Before shipping
Render the new piece CLOSE (2-4 m) from three angles and wide, and check each rule above. Write in the NOTES entry which rules were checked.
